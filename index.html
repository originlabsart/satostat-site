The pitch

The world's problem, and the radiator that pays you to fix it.

Satostat started as a patented six-board mining radiator built in the UK during the 2022 energy crisis. The silicon has changed to Bitcoin ASICs; the problem it was built for has not.

The problem

Heating is unaffordable

As of 2022, more than a third of Britons could not afford to heat their homes to a comfortable level. Every winter since, the gap between tariffs and incomes has stayed open.

Fossil fuels drive warming

Gas boilers and fossil generation release vast amounts of harmful by-products. Domestic heat is one of the last large sectors still to be electrified.

Business bills keep rising

Commercial property owners continue to see energy costs climb, the most visible impact of the energy crisis. Heat is a cost line with no return.

The solution

Same heat, plus income

The same or better heating than a traditional electric radiator, while the unit earns Bitcoin rewards for the household. Heat you were already paying for becomes heat with a rebate.

No fossil fuel at the point of use

Fully electric. Paired with rooftop solar or a green tariff, the heat and the hashing are both zero-carbon at the home.

Commercial heat that pays back

For offices, workshops and communal areas, rewards offset the cost of warming the space. Multi-unit fleets report energy and earnings per room.

Thermal architecture

Five layers between the silicon and your skin.

The radiator is a pressed galvanised-steel shell around a vertical air channel. Two rows of six fans push intake air up across the hashboard stack and out through the top vent, so the room fills with warm air from the top down.

A
Pressed steel front

Galvanised sheet pressed as one piece, powder coated. Top slot is the only exit vent, so convection carries the air column upward.

B
Aluminium fin stack

Extruded fins bonded to the hashboard heatsinks spread the load so the front surface sits in the 48–68 °C band and never spikes.

C
Six hashboards

Three sets of two. The number of active boards is the primary heat control; fan speed is the secondary. Boards hot-swap through the rear service hatch.

D
Two rows of six fans

Moulded into the rear bracket. Row B pushes across the array, row A pulls up to the vent. 120 mm, fluid bearing, PWM.

E
Rear bracket and power cage

Wall-mount rail, PSU bay, Ethernet and mains entry. The air-compliant cage holds the boards 25 mm off the shell.

Why public funders should care

  1. Heat that pays a rebate directly to the household, with no means test or voucher process.
  2. Fully electric. Every unit installed is a gas appliance not installed.
  3. Every unit reports its own energy use and heat delivered, so outcomes are measured, not estimated.
  4. Plug-in installation. No boiler removal, no wet system, no electrician visit.
  5. UK-designed, with a patented design and a UK assembly plan.

What we are asking for

  1. One housing provider to host a monitored pilot of 20 to 50 units for one heating season.
  2. Grant support for the units and the metering, matched by Satostat's development spend.
  3. Independent measurement of heat output, electricity use and household earnings.
  4. A published report that the national schemes can use to judge the technology.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Is it as warm as a normal electric radiator?

Yes, watt for watt. All electrical resistance heaters and all mining ASICs convert electricity to heat at effectively 100 %. An 800 W QuantumRad puts the same heat into the room as an 800 W panel heater. The difference is the vertical airflow design, which fills the room from the top faster than a passive panel.

Will it pay for my heating?

It offsets part of it. The calculator above uses your own assumptions. With typical UK tariffs and current network difficulty the rewards cover a portion of the electricity, not all of it. Treat the rewards as a discount on heat you were going to buy anyway, not as an income.

How loud is it?

The fans are 120 mm fluid-bearing units run at low RPM because the air channel is tall and the fin stack is large. Design targets are under 28 dB(A) in ECO and under 40 dB(A) in MAX at one metre, which is quieter than a fridge.

Do I need to understand Bitcoin?

No. The app sets up a wallet on your phone, picks a pool and starts hashing when the room needs heat. If you already run a node or prefer a specific pool you can point the unit at it in settings.

Does it need the internet to heat?

No. Without a connection it behaves as a plain electric radiator and follows its last schedule. Mining resumes when the connection returns.

Who holds the Bitcoin?

You do. Payout addresses are generated on your phone and written to the unit's secure element. Satostat never custodies funds and cannot see your balance.

What about summer?

Set the thermostat to a summer target and the unit idles, or switch it off entirely. Mining only runs when the room wants heat; there is no mode that heats a room you did not ask to heat.

Can it be installed in social housing or commercial space?

That is the pilot programme. The unit mounts on a standard rail, plugs into a 13 A socket and reports energy use per room, which makes it straightforward for housing providers to monitor.
